We collect administrative court rulings in residence-legalisation cases. Every entry can be
checked in [CBOSA](https://orzeczenia.nsa.gov.pl/).

## What these numbers say

**When the court assesses a case on the merits, the applicant wins in almost 9 cases out
of 10** ({{< orz merits_won >}} of {{< orz merits_all >}}). That is the real answer to
"is it worth going to court".

And since October 2025, when the courts followed the NSA line and stopped applying the
art. 100d suspension directly, it is better still: **{{< orz since_pct >}} of merits cases
won** ({{< orz since_won >}} of {{< orz since_all >}}). The courts that changed are exactly
the ones that used to dismiss most — Wrocław and Gliwice. We explain what happened in
[the guide to art. 100d](/poradniki/art-100d-zawieszenie-terminow/).

But half the cases in this database **never reach a decision on the merits** — they end in
a procedural ruling, and then the applicant never wins. Here is the important part: those
losses mostly do not mean the court sided with the office. They come from avoidable
procedural mistakes — the most common ones are in the table below the list of rulings.

The court fee is **100 zł**, and if your finances are difficult you can apply for legal aid
(*prawo pomocy*). A reminder (*ponaglenie*) is a precondition for the complaint. Both
obstacles can be cleared, and we describe both step by step:

- [Reminder template](/wzory/ponaglenie-bezczynnosc/) — file it **before** going to court
- [WSA complaint template](/wzory/skarga-wsa-przewleklosc/) — covering the fee and legal aid

{{< notatka >}}
**Being straight about this data.** We publish only rulings with a link to CBOSA — entries
without a verifiable source, and those we flagged as needing review, were left out. The
database is still being filled in, so **this is not complete statistics for all cases**.

Many cases are heard in **simplified proceedings**, where the court publishes only the
operative part with no reasoning — regardless of whether the applicant won or lost. The
description we give for such rulings is **our description of the outcome, not a summary of
the court's arguments**.

The outcome classification is our own assessment, not a quote from the ruling. A high share
of merits wins does not mean your case is won — only that courts in these cases usually
side with applicants. Read a ruling in CBOSA before relying on it.
{{< /notatka >}}
