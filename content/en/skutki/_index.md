Delay is not merely an inconvenience. It has two consequences, and neither stops at
foreigners.

**The first is corruption.** When a decision takes two years, a market appears: for
promises to speed things up, for "contacts in the office", for intermediaries.

And here begins the part that concerns everyone. **Corruption rarely stays where it
started.** An office does not have one honest counter for citizens and a separate,
purchasable one for foreigners — it is the same officials, the same hierarchy, the same
intermediaries "invoking their influence". A mechanism that has worked once in one queue
does not disappear when the type of case changes; on the contrary, it demonstrates that it
can be done and that nobody caught it.

An office where a faster decision can be bought stops being an office where the law means
the same thing for everyone. That is the foundation of the rule of law — and it is lost by
**everyone living in this country, whatever passport they hold**.

**The second is broken lives.** A frozen bank account, a lost job, a journey to a sick
parent made impossible, a family kept apart, a mortgage that cannot be paid. These are not
side effects — they are the real price people pay for a case left sitting.

This section collects both — as far as it can be documented.

{{< notatka >}}
**We publish documented cases only** — ones that can be checked: indictments, court
rulings, statements from the prosecutor's office or the CBA, reporting by named
newsrooms. Every entry cites its source.

We do not publish unverified accusations against specific people or offices. Not because
we disbelieve people, but because defamation is a criminal offence in Poland
(art. 212 of the Criminal Code), and a single unverified claim would undermine the
credibility of everything gathered here.
{{< /notatka >}}

## How much of this is visible

What we have gathered here is a small part of the cases that reached the media — and those
are in turn a small part of what actually happens.

Most such stories are never reported, for reasons that are easy to understand: reporting a
bribe means admitting you paid one, the residence case is usually still pending, and fear
of the consequences for your own situation outweighs any hope of justice. A person waiting
for a decision has the most to lose and the least reason to speak up.

The second filter is the media. The story of someone who could not sign an employment
contract for a year and a half is rarely "interesting" enough to become an article — and
when it concerns a foreigner, it meets a further barrier of imagination. Most Poles have
never had reason to learn what living on a stamp means: that it is not about
inconvenience, but about a bank account, a tenancy agreement, travelling to a funeral, the
right to work. Without that context such a story looks like a minor administrative matter
— and goes unwritten.

That is why the cases that reach the media are above all the ones with arrests and
charges. The quiet ruin of individual lives has no such threshold to clear.

**This section shows the tip, not the scale.**
