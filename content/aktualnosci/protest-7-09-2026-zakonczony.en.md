Our heartfelt thanks to everyone who came to stand up for their rights. Without you none
of this would have happened. **Between 200 and 300 people** gathered outside the Pomeranian
Voivodeship Office — and we are glad that so many people are ready to act in practice,
not just in words.

More people came than had confirmed their attendance. Reports from the scene give the same
figure — over 200 people; [we have collected the coverage at the end](#media-on-the-protest).

## The petition was handed over

The petition prepared for the protest was signed by a large number of people and handed to
the first vice-voivode [Emil Rojek](https://www.gov.pl/web/uw-pomorski/emil-rojek2).

It contained **11 points** from [the protest demands](/postulaty/) — those that fall within
the competence of the Pomeranian Voivodeship Office. The remaining **4 points** concern
nationwide matters: the repeal of art. 100d, legislative changes and digitalisation. They
are addressed to the government and the Sejm, not to the Voivode.

## First results

- The vice-voivode promised **24 new posts** in the department for foreigners, to speed up the handling of cases.
- A **working meeting** with the office has been agreed, to discuss the matters we raised.

A promise of staffing changes nothing by itself. What matters is what kind of posts these
will be — people who conduct cases and issue decisions, or front-desk service — when they
start work, and how that shows up in the waiting times. We will ask these questions at the
working meeting and report what we hear.

## What comes next

We know that one assembly will not solve this problem. But it is clear that things have
moved: the office used to simply ignore us, and now it is making contact and declaring
a willingness to talk.

The problem built up over years and one decision will not change it. It is also nationwide
— 4 of our demands did not even make it into the petition to the Voivode, because they fall
outside his competence.

We will report on how things develop and launch further actions.

## What you can do right now

Not everyone has the time and resources to act. But even the smallest steps, taken by many
people, matter enormously.

- **Join our Telegram group** — [@tu_zyjemy](https://t.me/tu_zyjemy), that is where we plan the next steps.
- **File a reminder (*ponaglenie*)** once the statutory deadline has passed — [template and instructions](/wzory/ponaglenie-bezczynnosc/).
- **Take inactivity to court** — [template complaint to the WSA](/wzory/skarga-wsa-przewleklosc/). It works: in our database, [96% of cases examined on the merits](/orzeczenia/) since October 2025 ended in the complainant's favour.
- **Insist on a certificate on time** — it has its own seven-day deadline: [how to do it](/poradniki/zaswiadczenie-w-sprawie-pobytowej/).
- **Follow our actions** and join them.

Another initiative worth supporting:

- **[Pobytrack](https://t.me/pobytrack)** collects data on how long proceedings really take. It is exactly this kind of data that lets us and other initiatives talk to officials concretely about the scale of the problem — we cite figures from the project in our [media materials](/dla-mediow/) too.
